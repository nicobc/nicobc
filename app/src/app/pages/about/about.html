I wrote my first lines of code over a decade ago, crunching numbers for an economics degree. This led to a career in data engineering. Today I build data platforms, and I've found that the hardest problems are rarely technical: KISS and YAGNI apply as much to architecture as to code.

Quality at scale requires maintenance, in systems and in the teams that build them. I was teaching before I was an engineer, and the reasons have only grown since. Team quality sets a ceiling on everything you ship. It's not only about the team though — teaching is also how I learn.

I believe engineering is the discipline of making intent machine-legible. Machines are literal; intent is invariably underspecified. Good engineering is a process of actively clarifying what's being asked, navigating constraints, and translating it such that the machine does what you meant. Implementation is where that translation is decided. Questions are where it starts. If you have one, reach out.
